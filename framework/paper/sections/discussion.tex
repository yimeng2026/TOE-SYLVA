\subsection{Caliber discipline as the central thesis}
\label{sec:thesis}
The paper's transferable claim is not the specific numbers of one
library but a methodological proposition: \emph{in a formal library
that mixes human and machine authorship, a proof-progress metric is
only meaningful as a (number, caliber) pair, and the caliber must be
declared, machine-checkable, and enforced by the same CI that enforces
the rest of the honesty layer.} The two self-caught incidents are the
evidence that this is load-bearing rather than ceremonious: the
\pnum{30.2\%} contamination alarm (F3) was produced by a caliber
mismatch that any of the library's own automated gates could have
made silently permanent had the false positive not been audited to
closure; the 25$\times$ dashboard disagreement (F2/C3) survived in
production documents for days precisely because both files carried
the same informal label (``honest proof rate'') with different
implicit calibers. A reviewer or meta-scientist who accepts only one
claim from this paper should accept this one.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Numbers are not yet canonical.} Six caliber conflicts
(C1--C6) are under P0 reconciliation; every number here is a declared
v7.98 baseline. The paper is un-submittable in this state by design
--- the skeleton exists so that the writing and the number
reconciliation can proceed in parallel without the former quietly
hard-coding the latter.
\item \textbf{No containerized reproducibility artifact.} Compile
logs exist and are three-way consistent (lake-manifest vs.\ two
independent builds), but carry local paths; a Docker/CI artifact
package is P1-8.
\item \textbf{Metatheorems missing.} The conservativity of G1--G3
transformations is asserted at the governance level, not formalized.
\item \textbf{Single-library case study.} The taxonomy was induced
from one library; cross-library validation (mathlib, AFP, or another
AI-generated corpus) is future work.
\item \textbf{Compile-log provenance.} Independent compilation was
performed on one machine class; the \pnum{6.7}\,s compile time is an
environment-dependent anecdote, not a benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anticipated objections}
\label{sec:objections}
% mapping of the four objection classes from D1 review 1.4-9
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{``AI-generated content cannot be trusted.''} The paper's
position: precisely --- which is why every artifact class carries a
registration-and-audit trail, and why the two metric defects were
caught by the loop itself. The claim is not that the library is
trustworthy; it is that its trust posture is \emph{explicit}.
\item \emph{``Your axioms are non-standard.''} The taxonomy exists to
separate genuine primitives from debt; the debt is being retired with
graded honesty; the residual is declared, not hidden.
\item \emph{``The batch corpus is contamination.''} Full-population
audit: code-level contamination is exactly one file, now quarantined;
the other \pnum{36198} string hits are denials, and the corpus's
triviality is a \emph{labeled property} of the corpus, not a hidden
one.
\item \emph{``Your headline numbers drifted by 25$\times$.''} Yes ---
and the drift is the finding: undecalared calibers, not dishonest
arithmetic. The conflict inventory and the placeholder discipline in
this very draft are the corrective.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future}
Three lines, in dependency order: (i)~\textbf{metatheorems} ---
formalize conservativity of the G1--G3 transformation classes
(the infrastructure target is a Lean proof that each transformation
extends the ambient theory conservatively); (ii)~\textbf{TrivialBench
sampling under the code caliber} --- the revised A-2 clause ships with
this program, and its first full sampling round will exercise the
quarantine list; (iii)~\textbf{reproducibility package} ---
containerized builds publishing \texttt{\#print axioms} artifacts per
release. Longer term, the registry + redemption + caliber-discipline
stack is library-agnostic; porting it to an external corpus is the
natural validation study.

\subsection{Ethics and transparency}
\label{sec:ethics}
The library and this paper were produced with AI assistance under
human tasking and review. The repository's transparency conventions
(AI-assistance markers on every artifact, deleted-claim ledger,
no-silent-deletion rule, CI-enforced honesty markers) apply to this
manuscript: the draft status, baseline numbers, and pending
reconciliation are visible by construction, and the supplementary
material includes the build log of the exact \LaTeX{} compile that
produced the accompanying PDF.
