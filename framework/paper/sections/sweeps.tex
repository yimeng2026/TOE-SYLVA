\subsection{The sweep protocol}
\label{sec:sweep-protocol}
A \emph{redemption sweep} is an organized pass over a work queue of
registered axioms, with four standing rules that every sweep report
must satisfy (learned, in part, from early violations the audits
later caught):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Registration first:} only registry entries are
redeemed; any axiom discovered mid-sweep that is not registered is
registered before it is touched.
\item \textbf{No silent deletion:} no entry disappears; each is
converted to something whose trust status is explicit (theorem with
proof, definition, or conditional theorem), and the sweep report
records the per-entry mapping axiom-name $\to$ new form $\to$
method.
\item \textbf{Semantics preserved or explicitly weakened:} the
statement of a redeemed axiom may not be quietly changed; if the
only honest conversion is a conditionalization (class \ding{172}
below), the conversion must be visible in the signature itself.
\item \textbf{Ledger closure:} the sweep report's own arithmetic
must close against the registry delta --- the rule whose early
violations became conflicts C5 and C6
(Sec.~\ref{sec:conflicts}).
\end{enumerate}

Six sweeps ran between 2026-08-18 and 2026-08-21 (repository
versions v7.94 through the v7.99 working tree), closing
$30+44+31+29+30+32 = 196$ registered axioms --- the canonical sweep
accounting. Sweep-level facts are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:sweeps}; the full per-entry records are the sweep
reports cited therein~\cite{sylvasweeps2026}.

\subsection{The five semantic classes}
\label{sec:fiveclasses}
The sweep reports count \emph{closures}. The program's central
bookkeeping decision is that a closure is not a unit of progress;
its \emph{semantic content} is. Every closure is therefore
re-graded, post hoc and independently of the sweep that produced it
(the grading was performed by the P0 reconciliation pass), into one
of five classes (plus one excluded category):

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\ding{172}] \textbf{Vacuous placeholder closure} (50 entries,
25.5\%). The axiom's conclusion was \texttt{True}; the closure
proves it by \texttt{trivial} (or \texttt{intro}; \texttt{trivial}).
The theorem now type-checks, is not an axiom, and says nothing.
This is honest bookkeeping: the placeholder debt is marked paid
\emph{at its true value, zero}.
\item[\ding{173}] \textbf{\boldmath$P\!\to\!P$ conditionalization}
(90 entries, 45.9\%). The axiom asserted $P$; the closure is
\texttt{theorem h : P} with hypothesis \texttt{(h : P)} and proof
\texttt{:= h}. The axiom disappears from the count, the statement
remains available to downstream consumers in conditional form, and
\emph{no mathematical content has been transferred}. We return to
the honesty of this class in Secs.~\ref{sec:pp} and
\ref{sec:limitations}.
\item[\ding{174}] \textbf{Data-to-definition conversion} (17
entries, 8.7\%). Axioms asserting numeric facts about physical
constants become \texttt{def}s: e.g.\ the critical density, asserted
as an axiom with a numeric value, becomes
\texttt{def rho\_c := 3*H0\^{}2/(8*$\pi$*G)} with the numeric value
retained in the docstring as data. The axiom footprint shrinks by
construction; the ``theorem'' purchased is definitional, not
mathematical.
\item[\ding{175}] \textbf{Definitional reconstruction} (24 entries,
12.2\%). Axioms whose statements are definitional equalities become
theorems proved by \texttt{rfl} after the underlying \texttt{def}
is repaired (constants previously given as bare numbers are
redefined by their formulas). Genuine proof work, of exactly zero
mathematical depth --- but it \emph{is} the correct fix, and it
survives \texttt{\#print axioms}.
\item[\ding{176}] \textbf{Substantive proof} (13 entries, 6.6\%).
Real proofs of real statements: identifications via mathlib lemmas
(e.g.\ \texttt{Real.rpow\_pos\_of\_pos} under an explicitly added
physical hypothesis $\alpha' > 0$), algebraic identities by
\texttt{ring}/\texttt{nlinarith}, existence constructions by
\texttt{exists\_intro}. Thirteen of 196.
\item[\ding{177}] \textbf{Axiom bundling, excluded from the
redemption classes} (2 entries, 1.0\%). Two closures restructured a
statement as a conjunction of two existing axioms (the Cook--Levin
reduction bundle; one analogy-of-science entry). The \texttt{axiom}
keyword disappears from the declaration count, but both ingredients
remain axioms --- so the P0 reconciliation reports these two
\emph{separately} from the five redemption classes: the canonical
sentence is ``196 closures $=$ five classes summing to 194, plus 2
bundling restructurings'', and no proof-rate or redemption claim
anywhere in the program counts them as content.
\end{enumerate}

Two features of Table~\ref{tab:sweeps} deserve emphasis. First, the
sweeps are \emph{specialized}: sweep2 eliminated the entire
placeholder class (all 44 remaining \texttt{True}-conclusion axioms
in its target set; the class total of 50 includes 6 from sweep1);
sweep3 was the definitional pass; sweeps 4--6 were progressively
more honest about what remained. Second, the composition drifts
\emph{down}, not up: the last two sweeps are 100\%
$P\!\to\!P$ --- not because the methodology degraded, but because
the registry queue was drained in order of decreasing semantic
availability, and what remains at the bottom is exactly the
material that cannot be redeemed honestly without doing or
importing mathematics.

\subsection{The honesty of \boldmath$P\!\to\!P$}
\label{sec:pp}
The $P\!\to\!P$ class deserves its own subsection because it is
the methodology's most easily abused move. The conversion
\begin{center}
\texttt{axiom X : P}
\quad$\longrightarrow$\quad
\texttt{theorem X (h : P) : P := h}
\end{center}
is a real improvement in three specific senses: the ambient logic
no longer \emph{assumes} $P$; downstream consumers that cite
\texttt{X} must now supply $P$ as a hypothesis, so the debt is
propagated instead of hidden; and the statement's trust status is
visible in its own signature. It is a nullity in the sense that
matters to a mathematician: no proof of $P$ has been produced, and
a proof-rate metric that counted 90 such conversions as 90 proofs
would be lying with precision.

The program's rule, enforced nowhere but in the taxonomy itself, is
that $P\!\to\!P$ conversions are only acceptable when (i) the
statement is retained verbatim as the conclusion, (ii) the sweep
report names the class explicitly, and (iii) the aggregate reporting
(the five-class table, never the bare count) keeps the class
visible. Sweep6 documented the limiting case honestly: some
registered propositions are \emph{false} under the corpus's stub
definitions (e.g.\ a partition-size identity that holds only for
$N=1$ under its stub, and a positivity statement that fails under a
zero-stub), and for those, $P\!\to\!P$ is not merely the cheap
conversion but the \emph{only} honest one --- asserting the
unconditional $P$ was the original defect. Sweep6's report also
discloses an operational constraint: the sweep was executed in an
environment without a Lean toolchain, so its conversions carry
structural verification (keyword, hypothesis, proof-body, verbatim
conclusion) but not compilation --- a boundary stated in the report
itself rather than papered over.

\subsection{Closing the declaration account}
\label{sec:evolution}
The six sweeps, plus non-sweep removals (template axioms deleted
from generated-but-committed files during corpus hygiene),
produce a monotone evolution of the live declaration count
(\texttt{axiom} commands in \texttt{sylva\_formalization/},
statement-level, canonical caliber):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Version & \texttt{axiom} declarations & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
v7.94 (registry baseline) & 445 & --- \\
v7.95 & 399 & $-46$ (sweep2: 44; non-sweep: 2) \\
v7.96 & 368 & $-31$ (sweep3) \\
v7.97 & 315 & $-53$ (sweep4: 29; non-sweep: 24) \\
v7.98 & 285 & $-30$ (sweep5) \\
working tree (v7.99) & 253 & $-32$ (sweep6) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The account closes exactly: $445 - 166\ (\text{sweep}_2..6) - 26\
(\text{non-sweep}) = 253$, matching the registry-residual and
live-declaration calibers independently
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reconciliation}). It is worth stating what this
arithmetic does and does not certify: it certifies that
\emph{entries} left the axiom ledger by declared, auditable routes;
it does not certify that mathematics happened --- for that, one
needs the five-class column, and there the answer is 13.
