\subsection{Corpus anatomy}
\label{sec:corpus}
The audited object is the \emph{non-batch} Lean corpus: all
\texttt{.lean} files under \texttt{sylva\_formalization/} except
the generated \texttt{SYLVA\_Proven*} batch modules (batch corpus:
\pnum{119859} files, governed separately,
Sec.~\ref{sec:quarantine}). The non-batch corpus is handcrafted in
the sense of ``curated by humans with AI assistance'', covering
physics-adjacent mathematics: Chern--Simons theory and the chern
number family, gauge theory and instanton constructions, quantum
Hall effect modules, cosmology (FLRW, dark energy, inflation),
string/M-theory brane content, computability (Cook--Levin, CNF
encodings, polynomial-time closures), $p$-adic analysis, a course
chain in algebraic number theory, and collective-intelligence and
learning-theory statements. Alongside it, the Agda track holds 7
files (Quantum, Spectrum, Complete, CNFCategory, Order, Cauchy,
Field modules) with 131 name-level \texttt{postulate} declarations
--- canonical caliber; the per-module split is Quantum~53,
Spectrum~36, Complete~15, CNFCategory~12, Order~12, Cauchy~2,
Field~1.

The asymmetry that motivates a registry: in this corpus, an
\texttt{axiom} command is not a mathematical foundation choice but
a \emph{deferred obligation} --- typically an AI-drafted statement
block that reads like a theorem, type-checks like a theorem, and
imports the full trust of the ambient logic the moment anyone builds
on it. Nothing in the Lean toolchain distinguishes ``foundational
axiom we choose to assume'' from ``proof we hope to write later''.
The registry exists to restore that distinction.

\subsection{The four-class taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy}
Every \texttt{axiom} declaration in the non-batch corpus is
registered in a machine-readable JSON registry
(\texttt{framework/axiom\_registry.json}, v2.0~\cite{sylvaregistry2026}) with per-entry
fields: file path, declaration name, statement, a taxonomy category,
and (where applicable) a suggested reduction method. The taxonomy
has four classes, defined by \emph{why the axiom exists} rather than
by subject matter:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Primitive} (250 entries): statements intended as
genuine mathematical content --- conjectures, physical input,
or results whose proofs are currently out of reach (e.g.\ the
spectral-action or Chern--Weil statements). These are the corpus's
real debts.
\item \textbf{Definitional} (142 entries): axioms that restate
definitions --- the ``proof'' is a definitional unfolding that
nobody performed (e.g.\ a physical constant asserted as an axiom
when it is the \emph{definition} of that constant).
\item \textbf{Placeholder} (42 entries): statements whose conclusion
is \texttt{True} (or an implication into \texttt{True}) --- pure
syntactic placeholders, carrying no mathematical content at all.
\item \textbf{Schema} (14 entries): cross-cutting structural
assumptions that license a whole family of statements at once
(e.g.\ the causal-network$\to$gauge-level identification of the
Chern--Simons chain, Sec.~\ref{sec:cschain}); tracked separately
because redeeming them is a research program, not a proof task.
\end{itemize}

The registry carries 448 entries over 116 non-batch files, with 391
unique names and \pnum{57} cross-file duplicate registrations
(canonical total; the decomposition 250/142/42/14 and the
residual identities of Sec.~\ref{sec:reconciliation} are canonical
per the P0 report). Version~2.0 of the registry also carries an
erratum trail in its metadata --- e.g.\ an early note asserting
``no \texttt{.agda} files exist'' was corrected by the P0
reconciliation, which measured the Agda track directly (7 files,
131 postulates) --- because the registry is itself subject to the
audit discipline it enforces.

\subsection{What the registry buys}
\label{sec:registry-value}
Three things, each exploited in later sections:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A census that cannot lie by aggregation.} The four
classes are disjoint, machine-tallied, and reproducible from the
registry JSON; ``how much does the library owe?'' acquires a
defensible answer (448 at baseline: 250 primitive, 142
definitional, 42 placeholder, 14 schema) instead of the previous
unbounded ``350+'' (an early headline later shown to be a
caliber artifact, conflict C1, Sec.~\ref{sec:conflicts}).
\item \textbf{A work queue with honest exit criteria.} Placeholder
axioms can be discharged by trivial proofs \emph{without changing
any semantics}; definitional axioms can be discharged by
refolding them as \texttt{def}s; primitives cannot be ``swept'' at
all and must either be proved, conditioned, or left standing with
their debt visible. This grading is what made the sweep program
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sweeps}) something other than number farming.
\item \textbf{An anchor for reconciliation.} Registry, live
declarations, and process logs are three independent records of the
same corpus; closing them against each other
(Sec.~\ref{sec:reconciliation}) is only possible because the
registry pins one of the three to a machine-readable ground truth.
\end{enumerate}

The registry does \emph{not} judge mathematics. It records
obligations. The distinction between a deep primitive and a
vacuous placeholder is a statement about \emph{bookkeeping}, not
about importance; the physics-facing primitives that remain
unredeemed (239 at the time of writing, plus 14 schema --- the
canonical residual 253 of Sec.~\ref{sec:reconciliation}) are the
library's honest, visible debt.
