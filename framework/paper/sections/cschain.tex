\subsection{The claim and the chain}
\label{sec:cs-claim}
The program's flagship physical claim (CLAIM 1 in the registry)
identifies the fine-structure constant's inverse with a
Chern--Simons level number, $\alpha^{-1} \approx n_{\mathrm{CS}} =
137$, and froze that prediction in a blind registry before the
comparison was performed. Its falsifiability clause is explicit:
a first-principles prediction of $\alpha^{-1}$ accurate to
$\pm 0.01$ without loop-integer self-tuning would refute the
identification. The claim's support is a numerical pipeline, not a
proof; the governance question is where, along the chain of
statements between the physics and the arithmetic, machine-checked
mathematics actually reaches.

Table~\ref{tab:chain} answers. The chain from the physical
identity down to quotient-group arithmetic decomposes into six
levels (L5--L0), each an independently checkable statement. The
audit census found: two levels are genuinely compiled mathematics
(L0, L1); one level is a compiled but \emph{degenerate} theorem
(L3, true only because a placeholder definition hard-codes the
integer); one level is an unredeemed schema axiom (L4); the top
level is exactly the CLAIM of the blind registry (L5); and the
remaining level, L2 --- integrality of the stratified Chern number
under integer weights --- is the deepest link whose proof closes
without touching any unredeemed axiom.

The audit also censused the side chains that hang off the main
one: knot-theoretic identifiers (Witten--Jones type, \pnum{15}
\texttt{True := trivial} placeholders), instanton indices (AS
index, 2 standing axioms with conditionally compiled theorems),
quantum Hall identifiers (TKNN-type, \pnum{21} vacuous
placeholders), and the Chern-number family side files (axiom
carriers, all graded CLAIM). In aggregate the side chains carry
\pnum{80+} vacuous entries plus 2 axioms --- each an independent
mathematical infrastructure project (gap G4 below). None of them
supports CLAIM 1; the registry keeps them visible precisely so
that no reader mistakes their existence for evidence.

\subsection{The upgrade: what was proved, and how it is labeled}
\label{sec:cs-upgrade}

The CLAIM$\to$THEOREM upgrade was performed at L2, on the theorem

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\small
\texttt{theorem stratifiedChernNumber\_int\_weights}\;
$(n\,v : \mathbb{Z}^{\times k})$ :\\[2pt]
\quad $\uparrow(\text{stratifiedChernNumberInt}\; n\; v) =
\text{stratifiedChernNumber}\; \uparrow n\; \uparrow v$
\quad --- \emph{i.e.}\ $\uparrow\!\sum_\alpha n_\alpha v_\alpha =
\sum_\alpha \uparrow(n_\alpha)\,\uparrow(v_\alpha)$.}}
\end{center}

The selection was a governance decision, not a mathematical boast,
and the registered rationale has three parts.
\emph{First, position}: T3 is the deepest link at which the
statement closes without touching any unredeemed axiom --- the PFE
source paper itself concedes that its integrality lemma (6.2) had
only a numerical pipeline, and that all its theorems close at the
sum-structure layer. \emph{Second, evidence quality}: T3 was
compiled for real on 2026-08-10 (Lean 4.29.0, mathlib4 pinned at
\texttt{8a178386}, exit 0, 6.7\,s), with \texttt{\#print axioms}
returning only the standard trio \texttt{[propext,
Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} on all six theorems of the module;
the log is archived in the panel records~\cite{sylvacscompilelog}. \emph{Third,
delegation pattern}: the proof discharges the $\mathbb{Z}\to
\mathbb{R}$ cast through two named mathlib lemmas ---
\texttt{Int.cast\_sum} (casts commute with finite sums) and
\texttt{Int.cast\_mul} (casts commute with multiplication),
applied under \texttt{Finset.sum\_congr} --- following the same
named-lemma discipline as the Dedekind case study
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dedekind-zero}).

The proof is three rewrites long. That is not an embarrassment;
it is the point. The registration's value is the \emph{label}, not
the difficulty: the row in the claim registry now says THEOREM
and links, conjunct by conjunct, to (i) the compile log, (ii) the
axiom-trio certificate, (iii) the named mathlib delegation, and
(iv) an explicit non-degeneracy note distinguishing T3 from the
L3 placeholder theorem above it. A companion strengthening round
added the $\exists$-form corollary T3$'$ (matching the logical
shape of \texttt{chernSimonsLevelInteger} so the L2/L3 interface
is fixed), plus T6/T7 (integer-sector 137-degeneracy and
concentrated-weights forms) --- added under a
zero-new-vocabulary discipline, with the honest disclosure that
the strengthening round executed in a no-toolchain environment,
so the THEOREM grade rests on the compiled T3 alone; T3$'$/T6/T7
carry an awaits-independent-compile note (one \texttt{lake env
lean} command, single file, pure mathlib dependencies).

\subsection{What T3 is not: gaps and the rollback incident}
\label{sec:cs-gaps}

An internal mathematics-physics referee (the D1 review's R2)
graded the upgraded statement bluntly, and the grading is
preserved in the paper: T3 is a $\mathbb{Z}$-ring closure
identity --- the cast homomorphism commuting with sums and
products --- \emph{not} a topological integrality theorem. The
physically meaningful integrality (Chern--Weil:
$\langle c_1(E), [M]\rangle \in \mathbb{Z}$, carried by a
cohomological integral) is nowhere in it; with integer weights the
integrality holds by construction. The corrected formulation,
which this paper adopts verbatim: T3 formalizes the
\emph{degeneration consistency} of the stratified formula on the
integer-weight sector and exhibits the precise sufficient
condition (integer weights) under which integrality holds;
real-weight integrality --- the physical target --- remains an
open gap. Any sentence that lets the reader hear ``T3 supports
$\alpha^{-1}=137$'' is a caliber error of the type this paper
exists to prevent; the blind-registry row keeps its CLAIM grade.

The case study also records an incident that no clean retrospective
would invent. The upgrade's registration (a THEOREM row in
\texttt{proof\_status.md}~\cite{sylvaproofstatus2026}) and the
T3$'$/T6/T7 additions were
performed by one session while a \emph{parallel} redemption sweep
was running; the sweep session, isolating what it believed were
foreign edits, rolled both back with \texttt{git checkout}. The
changes existed, then did not --- and the audit layer caught the
disappearance itself: a scheduled evidence check found the
registration missing, timestamps and \texttt{git status}
pinpointed the rollback, and the sweep report's own log (``$+60$
lines \ldots{} belongs to another session \ldots{} rolled back'')
confirmed the mechanism. Under the program's standard,
\emph{an uncommitted registration is a non-event}: the upgrade was
re-applied and committed with the next version. The incident
produced a standing rule --- registration files are serial-exclusive,
and no session rolls back another session's registry rows --- and it
is reproduced here because it is the honesty layer working as
designed, not as advertised.

Read as mathematics, then, the T3 upgrade is one small
cast-rearrangement lemma sitting under two admitted gaps and a
CLAIM it does not touch. Read as governance, it is the full
lifecycle: claim identification, chain census, deepest-provable
selection, compile evidence, delegation labeling, gap accounting,
and a rollback the audit layer itself detected. The story is in
the inventory audit trails, not the mathematics. The governance
contribution is the label: within this corpus, ``THEOREM'' now
means exactly ``compiled, zero-\texttt{sorry},
standard-axiom-trio, non-degenerate, with a named proof
delegation'', and the registry row points at the evidence for
each conjunct.
