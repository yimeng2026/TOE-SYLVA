\subsection{Large-scale formal libraries and their quality culture}
\label{sec:related-libraries}
The problems this paper addresses --- placeholder declarations,
metric drift, untracked deferred proof --- are not specific to
AI-assisted corpora, but AI assistance changes their \emph{rate} and
\emph{scale}. The established communities have long maintained
disciplines that our program independently rediscovers and then has
to formalize more aggressively, because at machine-generation scale
no informal convention survives.

\textbf{mathlib/Lean 4}~\cite{mathlib4,lean4} is the foundation layer
of our corpus and the reference point for axiom discipline. The
community norm is that a proof-development's axiom footprint is
inspectable with \texttt{\#print axioms}, and that a result relying
on nothing beyond Lean's standard axioms
(\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound})
is reported as such; that ``standard trio'' is exactly the
certificate our case-study registry requires
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cschain}). mathlib's own growth is curated through
review; our corpus deliberately contains an \emph{unreviewed} tier,
which is why we need a registry where a mature library needs code
review. Our claim-tier THEOREM* --- mathematics proved elsewhere,
formalized elsewhere, and only \emph{delegated to} by our corpus ---
is modeled on the ordinary situation of a downstream development
citing mathlib results; the repository's FLT registration (formal
Fermat's Last Theorem as delegated by mathlib, roughly
\pnum{12000}~lines of mathlib proof atop a large dependency cone ---
the figure is the repository's registration, not an independent
measurement) is the canonical example.

\textbf{Isabelle/HOL and the Archive of Formal
Proofs}~\cite{isabelle} pair a mature proof assistant with a
refereed, versioned archive: entries enter AFP through review and
are then kept building against the evolving Isabelle release ---
the closest existing analogue to our ``registration before
promotion'' rule, but with human referees where we use machine
audit plus tier labels. The AFP's review gate exists precisely
because unrestricted growth degrades trust; our batch corpus is the
limiting case of unrestricted growth, and its quarantine protocol
(Sec.~\ref{sec:quarantine}) is a machine-scale substitute for that
gate.

\textbf{Metamath}~\cite{metamath} represents the strictest tradition
of axiom economy: every proof step is an explicit substitution
instance of a small, fixed axiom set, so the notion of
``untracked trust'' cannot arise by construction. Our registry
taxonomy (Sec.~\ref{sec:taxonomy}) can be read as retrofitting a
weaker version of that strictness onto a corpus that was \emph{not}
built that way --- a retrofit that only became necessary once
AI-assisted drafting let statement volume outrun proof volume.

\subsection{LLM-generated proofs and autoformalization integrity}
\label{sec:related-llm}
A 2024--2026 literature line --- twelve papers whose identifiers
were verified in the repository's 2026-08-06 scan
\cite{leancopilot2024,forml4,leanworkbook,userintent2024,wild2025,
commonframework2025,masa2025,rlmeval2025,llmoutput2025,leanatlas2026,
paraphrase2026,robustness2026} --- frames
the exact failure surfaces this paper governs:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Assistance and generation.} Lean Copilot
\cite{leancopilot2024} and multi-agent formalization frameworks such as
MASA \cite{masa2025} put LLMs inside the proof-writing loop; corpora
built this way inherit the LLM's statement-level fluency without any
guarantee of proof-level content --- the precondition of failure
mode F1. Process-driven benchmarks (FormL4 \cite{forml4}) and
corpus-build methodologies (Lean Workbook \cite{leanworkbook},
common-framework proposals \cite{commonframework2025}) study how
NL$\to$Lean pipelines scale.
\item \textbf{Evaluation and its blind spots.} Research-grade
evaluation suites for real Lean projects (RLMEval \cite{rlmeval2025}),
in-the-wild autoformalization studies \cite{wild2025}, intent-capture
evaluation \cite{userintent2024}, and robustness studies showing that
frontier models fail under mere paraphrase \cite{paraphrase2026} or on
non-canonical real proof texts \cite{robustness2026} all converge on one
point: \emph{curated-benchmark performance does not transfer to
unguarded corpora}. Our batch corpus is precisely an unguarded
corpus, and Sec.~\ref{sec:sorry} shows what a naive scan of it
reports.
\item \textbf{Semantic hallucination.} The most directly relevant
result for us: type-checking success does not imply that a
formalized proposition faithfully expresses the intended mathematics
--- ``semantic hallucination'' in the terminology of the Lean Atlas
study \cite{leanatlas2026}, with formal verification of LLM-generated
outputs proposed as the mitigation \cite{llmoutput2025}. Our claim
tiers and falsifiability criteria
(Sec.~\ref{sec:tiers}) are a governance-scale answer to the
same problem: the registry records \emph{what} a statement claims
and at what tier, so that a type-checked placeholder cannot masquerade
as a theorem.
\end{itemize}

To our knowledge, the autoformalization literature documents the
\emph{capability} side of this problem extensively, but not the
\emph{corpus-governance} side: what to do once a mixed
human/AI library already contains four hundred trusted placeholders
and a hundred thousand generated modules. This paper is a case
study of the governance side.

\subsection{Audit precedents inside the repository}
\label{sec:related-audit}
The program did not start from zero: the repository's own history
supplied the audit precedent, and the paper's methodology is a
generalization of it. An earlier counting-geometry audit identified
and deleted 15 fabricated claims, logging them in a machine-readable
deleted-claims ledger
(\texttt{data/deleted\_fabricated\_claims.json}) together with the
protocol document that distilled the failure-mode taxonomy of
AI-generated mathematical writing. Four subsequent rounds of quality
engineering verified \pnum{1943} arXiv identifiers, zeroed
\pnum{25} failing verification scripts by fixing the underlying
content rather than loosening the assertions, and repaired some
\pnum{500} reference-text consistency defects
(DEVELOPMENT\_DIRECTIONS.md, repository history). The registry,
sweeps, and reconciliation machinery of
Secs.~\ref{sec:registry}--\ref{sec:reconciliation} is that practice
scaled from ``audit the papers'' to ``audit the library''; the
deleted-claims ledger survives as a CI-enforced invariant
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ci}).
