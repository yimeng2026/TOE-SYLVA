\subsection{Claim tiers and falsifiability}
\label{sec:tiers}
The governance layer begins with a vocabulary problem: the corpus
used ``formalized'' for statements ranging from textbook theorems
with complete machine-checked proofs to naked \texttt{axiom}
placeholders. The registry therefore fixes a four-tier vocabulary
(\texttt{proof\_status.md} sec 1~\cite{sylvaproofstatus2026}):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{11.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
Tier & Meaning \\
\midrule
THEOREM & machine-checked proof within the corpus (or complete
delegation to a checked external proof), core free of
\texttt{postulate}/\texttt{axiom}; certificate on file \\
THEOREM* & mathematical content proved and formalized
\emph{elsewhere}; the corpus delegates (e.g.\ FLT via mathlib) \\
CLAIM & reasoning, numerics, or citations exist, but no independent
formalization; falsifiability criteria mandatory \\
CONJECTURE & speculative, logically coherent, falsifiable \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two design decisions matter for the rest of this section. First,
the tiers are \emph{vocabulary for status, not for value}: a CLAIM
is not an embarrassment, it is a precisely delimited obligation
--- every CLAIM in the registry carries a falsifiability clause
(v7.24 discipline), e.g.\ for CLAIM 1: a first-principles
prediction of $\alpha^{-1}$ accurate to $\pm 0.01$ without
loop-integer self-tuning. Second, tier assignments are
\emph{evidence-linked}: each THEOREM row names the source file,
theorem, compile evidence, and proof chain (Secs.~\ref{sec:cschain}
and \ref{sec:dedekind-zero} showed the two hardest cases), and the
``key distinction'' printed under the registry table is the
program's motto: \emph{``formalized'' $\neq$ ``proved'' --- an
\texttt{axiom} is proof deferred to the future.}

\subsection{The honest proof-rate metric}
\label{sec:metric}
The registry's second pillar is a metric that refuses to count
deferred proofs as progress. The \emph{honest proof rate} is

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering
honest rate $=$ (theorems derived with zero \texttt{axiom}s, zero
\texttt{postulate}s, zero \texttt{sorry}s) $\div$ (all theorems
the corpus claims),\\
with handcrafted and batch content \textbf{reported separately} ---
merging them into one ``total rate'' is prohibited by the caliber
standard.}}
\end{center}

Under this metric the corpus's handcrafted tier scores 3.91\%
(22{,}328 of 571{,}611, independent dashboard caliber, 2026-08-11),
and the batch tier is listed separately at 0\% by construction.
The caliber history that led here is itself part of the finding:
an early 88.08\% line counted batch trivialities as proven
theorems; successive recalibrations reported 99.5\%, 99.9\%, then
100\% (the ``zero \texttt{sorry} per module'' caliber that ignored
\texttt{axiom}s entirely) --- all now formally deprecated ---
before the current definition landed. A number this mutable is
not a measurement; binding it to a named caliber
(Sec.~\ref{sec:calibers}) is what turns it back into one. The
normative reference implementation
(\texttt{compute\_proof\_rate.py},
\texttt{count\_sorry\_no\_comments}) counts code-level
\texttt{sorry}s only, comments stripped --- the same string/code
distinction that the next subsection dramatizes.

\subsection{The sorry incident: a 30.2\% contamination that
wasn't}
\label{sec:sorry}
In 2026-08-21, CI slimming analysis~\cite{sylvacislim2026} reported
that \pnum{30199} of
\pnum{119859} batch files (30.2\%) ``contained \texttt{sorry}'',
apparently contradicting the batch generator's zero-sorry design.
The contamination audit (full population, not sampling) reversed
the alarm:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.4cm}rrp{5.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
Pattern & Files & Share & Nature \\
\midrule
header negation statement & 36{,}198 & 99.997\% & fixed boilerplate
\texttt{Actual Lean 4 proofs --- no sorry, no True := trivial}
hit by substring search \\
name-level occurrence + trivial body & 1 & 0.003\% &
\texttt{SYLVA\_ProvenLayer2.lean}: theorem \emph{name} contains
``sorry'', and 8 of its 10 theorems are \texttt{True :=
trivial} \\
\midrule
code-level \texttt{sorry} (\texttt{\^{}$\backslash$s*sorry$\backslash$b},
comments stripped) & \textbf{0} & --- & the CI pattern's actual
check \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The single genuine offender was quarantined (below,
\cite{sylvaquarantine2026}), and the
string-caliber alarm was re-classified as a \emph{caliber-level
confusion}, not a contamination event --- the CI slimming report's
``hypothesis violated'' note was corrected accordingly. The audit
codified the three-level caliber standard that the reconciliation
chapter applies to every number: \textbf{string level} (substring
search --- never a valid basis for honesty claims),
\textbf{code level} (statement-level pattern, comments stripped
--- the reference caliber), \textbf{CI-pattern level} (the exact
regular expression the honesty gate uses). A side observation
with governance weight: under the \emph{string} caliber, filtering
for files that ``look honest'' would have deleted a
\pnum{14\%} slice of the Analysis domain --- caliber errors
are not cosmetic, they misallocate deletion decisions.

\subsection{Quarantine, not deletion}
\label{sec:quarantine}
The response to the one contaminated module is the repository's
template for handling genuinely bad content: \texttt{SYLVA\_ProvenLayer2.lean}
was moved to \texttt{releases/quarantine/} with a machine-readable
manifest (origin path, SHA-256, reason list, exclusion scope), the
batch census was updated from \pnum{119859} to
\pnum{119858} files, and its 10 template theorems were removed
from the metric's numerator and denominator alike. Nothing was
silently deleted; the no-silent-deletion rule (below) applies with
special force to content whose existence is embarrassing. The
quarantine directory is the batch corpus's equivalent of the
deleted-claims ledger: a place where failures remain visible,
attributable, and countable.

\subsection{The honesty CI and its explicit boundary}
\label{sec:ci}
The governance layer is machine-checked.
\texttt{verify\_honest\_repo.py --ci} gates every round: a
forbidden-term scan with deletion-context allowance (so that
\emph{deleting} a fabricated claim is never itself flagged), the
required honesty markers (\texttt{AI-assisted-generated} banners
on every report), the deleted-claims ledger
(\texttt{data/deleted\_fabricated\_claims.json}~\cite{sylvafabrication2026},
\pnum{15} entries from the counting-geometry fabrication audit,
well above the gate's 10-entry minimum), BibTeX integrity
checks, and new-paper presence checks. It passes in seconds
(exit~0), every round.

The gate's \emph{boundary is stated in the gate itself}: it
checks the honesty layer --- markers, ledgers, manifests,
caliber-discipline artifacts --- and deliberately does not check
Lean proof content (no \texttt{lake build}; the full build
exceeds the audit machines' \pnum{4GB}-class memory, and the Agda
side has never compiled in CI for the same reason). Conflating ``honesty CI green'' with ``library correct''
would be a caliber error of exactly the kind this paper catalogs;
the Lean side is instead pinned (mathlib4
\texttt{8a178386}, toolchain Lean 4.29.0, lakefile + manifest
committed) and carries independent per-module compile logs, while
the batch generator is reproducible from its manifest
(\pnum{119831} entries, SHA-256 sampling \pnum{500}, zero
mismatches). The remaining gap --- containerized, artifact-
publishing reproducibility --- is registered as future work
(Sec.~\ref{sec:future}).
