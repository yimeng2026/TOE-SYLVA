\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted formalization scales content faster than the human
communities of practice that traditionally guarantee it, and the debt
accumulates in three specific failure modes: axioms used as deferred
proofs, proof-rate metrics that drift with the measuring caliber, and
string-level scans that mistake comments for placeholders. We document
the axiom-governance program of TOE-SYLVA, a Lean~4 (+\,Agda) library
built on a pinned mathlib4 revision and containing \pnum{119859}
machine-generated batch modules alongside handcrafted developments.
% v7.98 baseline: 119,859 SYLVA_Proven* files in working tree
% (framework/sorry_contamination_audit.md sec 1; ci_slimming_phase1_report.md)
The program comprises: (i)~a four-class axiom taxonomy (primitive,
definitional, schema, placeholder) with a machine-readable registry of
\pnum{448} axioms;
% v7.98 baseline: 448 (axiom_registry.json v2.0; C1 in
% journal_submission_d1_review.md sec 1.3)
(ii)~a redemption methodology executed across six sweeps, whose
\pnum{196} closures we re-classify into four semantic grades --- vacuous
placeholder cleanup, trust transfer ($P\!\to\!P$ conditionalization),
definitional reconstruction, and substantive proof --- of which only
\pnum{13} carry non-trivial proof content;
% canonical (p0_reconciliation_report.md secs 2.1/4): 196 = 30+44+31+
% 29+30+32 (sweeps 1-6; definitional 142 and placeholder 42 classes
% fully cleared); 13 substantive = 6.6% of closures. Supersedes the
% double-counted "136+30=166" (2 v7.94 entries are inside sweep1's 30)
% and the irreproducible 598->568 caliber.
(iii)~an honest proof-rate metric that counts code-level \texttt{sorry}
with comments stripped, replacing a caliber history that drifted from
\pnum{88.08\%} to \pnum{100\%} and then collapsed to \pnum{3.91\%} once
axioms were no longer ignored;
% v7.98 baselines: caliber history per proof_status.md sec 5 and
% proof_rate_dashboard.md (2026-08-11); DASHBOARD.md conflict = C3
and (iv)~a four-tier external claim layer (THEOREM / THEOREM* / CLAIM /
CONJECTURE) with falsifiability criteria. Two incidents in which the
governance loop caught defects in its own metrics --- a \pnum{30.2\%}
``sorry contamination'' alarm that reversed to zero under a code-level
caliber, and a 25$\times$ disagreement between two dashboards measuring
the same library --- are elevated into the methodological thesis of the
paper: \emph{every proof-library metric must be bound to an explicitly
declared caliber level (string / code / CI-pattern)}, and the binding
must be machine-checkable. We close with a full lifecycle case study ---
a CLAIM$\to$THEOREM upgrade along a Chern--Simons dependency chain with
mathlib-delegation honesty labels --- and the quarantine of the single
genuinely contaminated module in the batch corpus.
\end{abstract}
