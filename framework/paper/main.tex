\begin{abstract}
\noindent AI-assisted formalization programs can scale proof-term
production faster than they scale proof \emph{verification}. We
report the governance methodology of TOE-SYLVA, a Lean~4 corpus
built on pinned mathlib4 that accumulated, alongside genuine
developments, a large ledger of \texttt{axiom} declarations used as
deferred-proof placeholders, plus a machine-generated tier of
119,859 modules. The methodology has four components.
(1)~\emph{Axiom audit}: a machine-readable registry of every
non-batch \texttt{axiom} (448 entries at baseline) with a
four-class taxonomy separating genuine mathematical assumptions
from definitional shorthand and vacuous placeholders.
(2)~\emph{Redemption sweeps with semantic re-grading}: six sweeps
closed 196 entries (30+44+31+29+30+32); the P0 reconciliation
re-grades them into five classes --- 50 vacuous placeholder
closures, 90 $P\to P$ conditionalizations, 17 data-to-definition
conversions, 24 definitional reconstructions, and 13 substantive
proofs (plus 2 bundling restructurings excluded from the content
count) --- an intentionally unflattering decomposition in which
71.4\% of closures transfer no proof content.
(3)~\emph{Three-caliber reconciliation}: registry (448),
live-declaration (253), and sweep-process (196) accounts are
reconciled to exact identities, and six documented caliber
conflicts (axiom counts, Agda postulates, dual proof-rate
dashboards disagreeing by 25$\times$, a 30.2\% ``sorry
contamination'' alarm that reversed to zero under a code-level
caliber, an internal ledger miscount, and double-counted
closures) are each resolved with named calibers.
(4)~\emph{An honesty layer}: claim tiers with falsifiability
criteria, an honest proof-rate metric (3.91\% = 22,328/571,611 on
the handcrafted corpus; batch tier reported separately at 0\%; a
legacy 99.79\% dashboard is preserved, marked stale), a quarantine
protocol, and a CI gate that fails on silent deletions. Two case
studies --- a CLAIM$\to$THEOREM upgrade along a Chern--Simons
chain with explicit mathlib-delegation labels, and a
Dedekind-domain segment honestly registered as zero-new-mathematics
delegation --- exhibit the full lifecycle. The transferable claim:
in mixed human/AI proof libraries, metrics are trustworthy only as
(number, caliber) pairs, with calibers declared, machine-checkable,
and CI-enforced.
\end{abstract}

\section{Glossary of Calibers}\label{sec:glossary}\label{sec:calibers}
\begin{itemize}
\item \caliber{String caliber}: substring match over raw file
content (comments included). Cheap, reproducible, and the source
of the 30.2\% false alarm. Never authoritative for placeholder
counts.
\item \caliber{Code caliber}: statement-level pattern with
comments stripped (e.g.\ \texttt{\^{}\textbackslash s*sorry\textbackslash b});
the reference level for placeholder scans.
\item \caliber{CI-pattern caliber}: the exact regular expressions
used by \texttt{verify\_honest\_repo.py -{}-ci}; the binding level
for gate behavior.
\item \caliber{Registry caliber} (axioms): entries of
\texttt{axiom\_registry.json} (file$\times$name pairs).
\item \caliber{Declaration caliber} (axioms): \texttt{axiom}
command census over \texttt{.lean} files (scope variants: corpus
only, or whole repository).
\item \caliber{Sweep caliber} (redemptions): per-entry closure
records in the sweep reports, summed.
\item \caliber{Name-level caliber} (Agda): postulate name
declarations, as distinct from top-level \texttt{postulate} blocks
or substring occurrences.
\end{itemize}
